Step 2 · The soil

No hectare is the same as the next one

The hilltop is well-drained loam. Lower down it becomes more clayey, and one corner has a sandy patch. Each texture holds water and nutrients differently.

Step 3 · Water

The terrain decides where water stays

In the lowland water pools, and on the sand dune it drains right away. The same crop behaves differently at each spot, even if it rains the same over the whole farm.

What is real and what is not

  • Real: each season's climate (NASA POWER, a reference point near Pergamino), yields by area, harvest prices and costs (MAGyP, September 2026 report).
  • Illustrative: the soil, terrain and pH of each hectare. We do not have real soil data for this farm; we built it to show how the decision changes.
  • The margin is gross, without land rent. Double-crop soybean uses the yield and price of soybean in general.
  • The “bad year” comes from a model that gets which years are bad right but underestimates how much yields drop. The real bad year can be worse.
  • The alternative rotations are the team's examples, not an optimization: they are ideas to discuss with an agronomist. This supports the farmer's decision, it does not replace it.
